# GAM-11 H20 — Sticking-region literature and convention mapping

Research date: 2026-09-11

This note records only primary study evidence used to contextualize the
event-based H20 measurements. It does not turn study means or standard
deviations into acceptance limits for the game.

## Primary sources

### Larsen, Kristiansen, and van den Tillaar (2021)

*New Insights About the Sticking Region in Back Squats: An Analysis of
Kinematics, Kinetics, and Myoelectric Activity.* Frontiers in Sports and Active
Living, 3, 691459. DOI: [10.3389/fspor.2021.691459](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2021.691459).

- Population: 25 recreationally trained lifters; 13 women and 12 men.
- Squat/load: high-bar back squat, 3-RM test.
- Event definition: `v0` is the lowest barbell height at the descent-to-ascent
  change; `vmax1` is the first maximal upward velocity; `dmax1` is the maximum
  deceleration inside `vmax1`–`vmin`; `vmin` is the first local minimum upward
  velocity; `vmax2` is the subsequent second peak. The sticking region is
  `vmax1`–`vmin`.
- Measurement: linear encoder at 200 Hz with five-point differential velocity;
  3-D motion capture at 500 Hz; model-based quantities low-pass filtered with
  a 10 Hz Butterworth filter; joint angles calculated distal-to-proximal with
  Cardan x-y-z order.
- Reported mean ± SD at `v0`, `vmax1`, `dmax1`, `vmin`, `vmax2` respectively:
  bar velocity `0`, `0.34 ± 0.07`, `0.21 ± 0.06`, `0.11 ± 0.06`,
  `0.82 ± 0.21 m/s`; bar displacement `0`, `0.06 ± 0.01`, `0.17 ± 0.04`,
  `0.24 ± 0.06`, `0.58 ± 0.06 m`; time `0`, `0.24 ± 0.06`, `0.52 ± 0.20`,
  `1.48 ± 0.29`, `2.80 ± 0.92 s`.
- Reported joint-angle means ± SD: torso `48.6 ± 11.5`, `51.9 ± 12.6`,
  `55.6 ± 13.1`, `56.4 ± 13.8`, `32.5 ± 8.8°`; hip flexion
  `111.8 ± 7.8`, `108.3 ± 7.8`, `101.6 ± 12.9`, `92.5 ± 11.4`,
  `49.6 ± 10.2°`; knee flexion `125.3 ± 6.6`, `115.2 ± 6.9`,
  `99.2 ± 11.5`, `84.7 ± 9.6`, `49.2 ± 5.6°`; plantar flexion
  `105.2 ± 5.4`, `101.9 ± 4.6`, `97.1 ± 6.2`, `93.2 ± 6.0`,
  `87.1 ± 4.8°`.
- Use in H20: event definitions, the existence of an event-defined region in
  a successful 3-RM sample, the qualitative pattern of increased torso
  inclination and changing hip/knee contributions around `dmax1`/`vmin`.

### van den Tillaar, Knutli, and Larsen (2020)

*The Effects of Barbell Placement on Kinematics and Muscle Activation Around
the Sticking Region in Squats.* Frontiers in Sports and Active Living, 2,
604177. DOI: [10.3389/fspor.2020.604177](https://doi.org/10.3389/fspor.2020.604177).

- Population: 10 healthy, trained male competitive powerlifters with at least
  three years of squat training.
- Squat/load: matched-depth, matched-stance high-bar and low-bar back squats at
  5-RM; successful load `158 ± 29 kg`.
- Event definition: lowest barbell point `V0`, first maximal upward velocity
  `Vmax1`, lowest velocity `Vmin`, and second maximal velocity `Vmax2`; the
  sticking region is `Vmax1`–`Vmin`.
- Measurement: 200 Hz linear encoder with five-point differential velocity;
  500 Hz 3-D motion capture; marker-line segment angles; final repetition used.
- Reported sticking-region displacement fractions were `12.2 ± 3.7%` at
  `Vmax1` and `39.7 ± 9.7%` at `Vmin` of total upward displacement.
- Reported low-bar angle means ± SD at `V0`, `Vmax1`, `Vmin`, `Vmax2`:
  ankle flexion `72 ± 13`, `76 ± 13`, `86 ± 13`, `92 ± 11°`; knee flexion
  `62 ± 12`, `75 ± 13`, `114 ± 17`, `141 ± 11°`; hip flexion
  `62 ± 28`, `69 ± 30`, `101 ± 30`, `133 ± 24°`.
- Use in H20: the closest population/technique context for a powerlifting
  squat, while retaining the paper's own angle labels rather than equating
  them to the game's joint-space twist.

### van den Tillaar, Andersen, and Sæterbakken (2014)

*The Existence of a Sticking Region in Free Weight Squats.* Journal of Human
Kinetics, 42, 63–71. DOI: [10.2478/hukin-2014-0061](https://doi.org/10.2478/hukin-2014-0061).

- Population: 15 healthy males, `82 ± 11 kg`, `179 ± 6 cm`, with
  `6 ± 3` years of resistance training.
- Squat/load: free-weight two-legged 6-RM squats. The participant/methods
  summary reports `135 ± 33 kg`; the Results section reports the lifted 6-RM
  load as `137 ± 28 kg`. These are the paper's differing overall summaries,
  not separate load values for the sticking and no-sticking groups.
- Event definition: the first maximal upward velocity to the first local
  minimum upward velocity; a participant need not display a clear sticking
  region. Ten of fifteen participants displayed one in the last repetition.
- Reported event means ± SD for the participants with a sticking region:
  velocity at `Vmax1`, `Vmin`, `Vmax2` `0.174 ± 0.056`, `0.142 ± 0.080`,
  `0.723 ± 0.159 m/s`; displacement from `V0` `0.067 ± 0.035`,
  `0.103 ± 0.043`, `0.371 ± 0.046 m`; knee angle `89 ± 12`, `98 ± 11`,
  `102 ± 11`, `136 ± 12°` at `V0`, `Vmax1`, `Vmin`, `Vmax2`; time intervals
  `0.380 ± 0.108`, `0.216 ± 0.100`, `0.707 ± 0.154 s`.
- Use in H20: evidence that a valid human 6-RM sample can lack a clear region;
  no-region is therefore not automatically a controller defect.

### van den Tillaar (2015)

*Kinematics and Muscle Activation Around the Sticking Region in Free-Weight
Barbell Back Squats.* Kinesiologia Slovenica, 21(1), 15–25. Stable repository:
[dLib record](https://dlib.si/details/URN:NBN:SI:doc-8BD93SCR?language=eng).

- Population: 11 resistance-trained males; `86.8 ± 21 kg`, `1.81 ± 0.08 m`.
- Squat/load: full free-weight 6-RM back squat; last repetition analyzed.
- Measurement: 3-D kinematics and EMG; the study relates the timing of ankle,
  knee, and hip angular-velocity extrema to the bar-velocity events.
- Use in H20: qualitative coordination context only; no unsupported conversion
  of its marker/segment angles into the game's internal joint coordinates.

### Larsen et al. (2022)

*Effects of Barbell Load on Kinematics, Kinetics, and Myoelectric Activity in
Back Squats.* Sports Biomechanics. DOI:
[10.1080/14763141.2022.2085164](https://doi.org/10.1080/14763141.2022.2085164).

- Population: 12 healthy males, `83.5 ± 7.8 kg`, `180.3 ± 6.7 cm`, able to
  squat at least 1.5 bodyweight for 1-RM.
- Squat/load: back squat at 90%, 100%, and 102% of 1-RM; reported successful
  loads `134 ± 17` and `149 ± 19 kg` at 90% and 100%, with failure at
  `153 ± 19 kg` at 102%.
- Event definition: `v0`, `vmax1`, `dmax1`, `vmin`; displacement is measured
  from `v0`. The 102% condition did not produce a post-sticking region.
- Measurement: encoder/motion-capture event analysis with kinematics, kinetics,
  joint moments, and EMG.
- Use in H20: load and failure-context evidence; no numerical acceptance band
  is imported because H20 loads are not matched to the study's 1-RM scale.

## Angle and frame reconciliation

| Quantity | H20 simulation definition | Literature convention/compatibility |
|---|---|---|
| Bar velocity | `Rigidbody.linearVelocity.y`, world +Y upward, m/s | Encoder vertical velocity; sign is mapped to +up before comparison. |
| `v0` position | Minimum sampled bar shaft-center y in the one-repetition trace | Lowest barbell height. |
| Knee/hip/ankle joint-space values | Calibrated `J_i` X-twist; zero is logical neutral; signs follow the GAM-10-to-physical mapping | Not directly comparable to marker-derived angle labels. |
| Included-angle proxies | Explicit `Vector3.Angle` between declared physical landmark vectors, degrees | Closest to marker-line/included-angle descriptions, but remain engineering proxies. |
| Trunk pitch | `atan2(Δz, Δy)` from pelvis to thorax; world vertical +Y, anterior +Z, positive forward | Papers report torso/trunk angle relative to laboratory/segment conventions; numerical equality is not assumed. |
| COP/support | Contact-point and solver-impulse estimate, world AP +Z | Human studies use force plates; H20 labels the game quantity as an engine contact estimate, not force-plate COP/GRF. |

The 2020 and 2021 papers do not use identical joint-angle labels or segment
conventions (their reported knee series even runs in opposite numerical
directions across events). H20 therefore compares event ordering, displacement,
velocity, timing, and qualitative coordination; any numerical angle comparison
is labelled as a convention-matched proxy, never as direct validation of the
game's internal joint twist.

## Claim boundary

These studies support the event vocabulary and provide contextual human
kinematic patterns. They do not validate the game's mass distribution, joint
drive gains, solver torque, modeled demand, COP estimate, muscle behavior, or
biological authenticity.
